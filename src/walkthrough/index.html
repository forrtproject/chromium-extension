Search results 2 of 9

Judge a result before you open it

On search sites, ORE adds a small panel beside each result to help you skim the results. Click a row to open the paper, copy its reference, find an open-access version, read the discussion, or see its replications. A row that says “Checking…” is still running; it does not mean nothing was found.

When a result doesn't show a DOI, ORE looks the title up in Crossref and OpenAlex and picks the closest match by author and year. A DOI found this way is underlined with dots.

Copy a ready-made reference
The quote icon copies the reference in your chosen citation style, formatting included, ready to paste into Word or Google Docs.
Check a dotted DOI before you rely on it
Two papers can have almost the same title. If the match is wrong, everything else in the panel describes the wrong paper. Open the DOI once to confirm.

Articles & other pages 3 of 9

On an article page, two things appear

A small pill appears beside the article's DOI, beside each paper in its reference list, and beside any other DOI mentioned on the page. A reference that cites two papers gets two pills.

A tab on the right edge of the window opens the Meta Report: everything ORE found, in one place. Drag it up or down if it covers something.

A pill means ORE recognised the paper
No pill on a reference means ORE could not identify it, so nothing was checked for it.
Turn it off where you don't want it
Click the ORE button in your browser toolbar to hide it on this page, snooze it on this site, or turn it off for the site entirely.

Reading the pill 4 of 9

Reading the pill

Each part of the pill answers one question. A coloured part means yes. A faded part means nothing was found yet, or the check could not be done; open the pill to see which.

Point at the pill to open it. Each part becomes a row with a link or action. Click to keep it open.

DOI — which paper this is
Copy the DOI or a formatted reference, or open the paper. A dotted underline means it was matched by title.
OA — an open-access version exists
Open Access: a legal open-access version is online, found through Unpaywall. The row opens the best version; the arrow beside it lists the others.
PubPeer — researchers are discussing it
PubPeer is a site where researchers comment on published papers, often to point out problems. The number is the comment count; the row opens the thread.
Reps — the study was repeated
A replication repeats the study with new data; a reproduction reruns the original analysis. The number counts replications, or reproductions when there are none; the row shows them in the FLoRA Replication Atlas.

Withdrawn & questioned work 5 of 9

Retracted, or under question

A retraction means the paper has been withdrawn, usually because of serious errors or misconduct. An expression of concern means the journal has flagged a problem that is not yet resolved; the paper still stands.

Either one adds a warning to the end of the pill and appears in the Meta Report. Both link to the journal's notice, so you can read the reason. The data comes from Retraction Watch.

Checked on your computer
ORE keeps its own copy of the retraction list and refreshes it weekly, so no server learns what you are reading.

Articles & other pages 6 of 9

The Meta Report: everything in one place

The side tab opens a report on the article you are reading: its replications and what they found, any retraction or concern, its PubPeer discussion, and which of its references have something to report.

If the article is itself a replication, the report shows the original study it repeated.

Treat outcome labels as a starting point, not a verdict on the original. They depend on how replication authors describe their results. A failed replication does not by itself show that the original finding was false or the original study flawed: a replication can also fail by chance, because of low power or problems in its own conduct, or because conditions, situations, or contexts differed.

What the FLoRA Replication Atlas is
A free database, run by the FORRT community, of studies in the social and behavioural sciences that tried to replicate or reproduce earlier findings, whatever the outcome, and what they found. Every replication count in ORE comes from it and links to the evidence.
Download or share it
Download saves the report as a PDF. Copy link shares the whole report in the link itself, so nothing is uploaded.

Spreadsheets & documents 7 of 9

Check a whole list of papers at once

Open a spreadsheet of DOIs, such as a reading list or the studies for a review, in Google Sheets or Excel Online. In Google Sheets, ORE checks every DOI in the tab, including rows you have not scrolled to. Excel Online only draws the rows on screen, so ORE checks those; scroll to cover the rest. Results appear in a popup.

Retracted papers are listed first, each with a link to its notice, and the popup appears for them even if nothing else was found.

One tab at a time
Each sheet tab is checked on its own. Closing the popup on one tab leaves the others alone.
Hide or snooze
Hide for this sheet closes the card for this sheet. Snooze hides it for ten minutes.
Writing in Google Docs or Word Online?
Each reference with a DOI gets a small marker. Open it for the same details as a pill.

Set up ORE

Your choices are saved as soon as you make them, and you can change them any time in Settings.

Needed to find open-access versions: Unpaywall asks for a contact address. ORE also gives it to Crossref, OpenAlex and NCBI when it looks papers up. Remove it any time.

The format the copy button uses: APA, Vancouver, BibTeX and eight more.

Where should ORE run?

ORE is on for journals, publishers, preprint servers and documents, and off for shopping, video, messaging and web search. Tick or untick a category, or open it to choose site by site.
